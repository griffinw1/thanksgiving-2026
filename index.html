Roast, flip & finish

Thu 11:15 AM →breast 150°Fthigh 165°F
  1. Oven to 425°F. Rack in the roasting pan. Lay 2–3 foil strips across it, each folded in half twice (~10 layers), and spray them. 1 cup water in the pan. 10:00
  2. Bird out of the brine (no rinse). Turn it breast-up in the bag and give each breast one more full injector. 9:43
  3. Into the pan breast-down on the foil strips. 10:31
  4. Probes: breast from the neck end, deep toward the tenderloin; thigh into the thickest part where it meets the drumstick. Neither touches bone. Cables out the door, display outside. Alarms 150 / 165. 11:52
  5. Double layer of foil over the top. Roast 1 hr at 425°F. 13:20
  6. Foil off, oven to 325°F. Roast until breast 150°F and thigh 165°F. For 18 lb expect ~2–2.5 hr at this stage (his 17 lb bird took ~3 hr total).
  7. Tent with foil + a towel. Rest 45–60 min (up to ~1.5 hr is fine).
  8. Oven to 500°F. Flip breast-up: tongs deep in the cavity, towel on the bird, turn 90°, regrip, turn again. Tie the drumsticks with twine; brush the breast with olive oil.
  9. Lift the bird, pour ~¾ of the drippings into the warm gravy. Taste. Add the rest only if it isn't salty yet.
  10. Back in at 500°F for 20 min until golden (splotchy is normal for brined birds). Rest 10 min.
  11. Carve: cut between breast and leg to the joint, press the leg down, follow the breastbone to lift each breast off, slice skin-side up.

  2. Boil the elbows in well-salted water, 1–2 min under the box time. Drain.
  3. Melt the butter over medium, whisk in the flour, cook 2–3 min until it smells nutty.
  4. Slowly whisk in the milk and cream. Simmer ~5 min, whisking, until it coats a spoon.
  5. Off the heat, stir in the 450 g cheddar and 450 g Gruyère a handful at a time. Add salt, pepper, Dijon, 1 tsp smoked paprika, and the optional hot sauce and garlic powder. Taste.

Skillet cornbread

Lance · Thu ~4:20 PM425°F~35 min totalVideo

Fluffy crumb with a crisp, lacy crust from a preheated, oiled pan. One 9" pan, about 8–10 wedges. Double it in a 12" skillet or 9×13 (+~10 min) if the table needs more.

IngredientAmountNote
Fine yellow cornmeal220 g1½ cups. Medium grind: 236 g
All-purpose flour90 g¾ cup
Granulated sugar50 g¼ cup
Baking powder2 tsp
Fine sea salt2 tsp
Whole milk, room temp360 mL1½ cups
Unsalted butter, melted56 g¼ cup
Large egg, room temp1
Vegetable oil1 tbspFor the pan, or bacon drippings
  1. Oven to 425°F. Use a 9" cast-iron skillet or metal cake pan; glass or ceramic won't crisp the edge.
  2. Whisk the dry: cornmeal, flour, sugar, baking powder, salt.
  3. Whisk the wet in a second bowl: milk, melted butter, egg.
  4. Once the oven is fully hot, put the oil in the pan and heat it in the oven 5 min.
  5. Make a well in the dry mix, pour in the wet, whisk only until the dry streaks are gone.
  6. Pull the hot pan and pour the batter in right away. It should sizzle.
  7. Bake 18–22 min, until a toothpick in the center comes out clean (10" skillet or 9×9: check at 15).
  8. Rest 5 min in the pan, then flip it out onto a wire rack so the crust stays crisp.
  9. Cool 5–10 min before cutting; sliced hot, the crumb goes gummy.
Notes. Weigh the cornmeal; scooping packs it and bakes up dry. Buttermilk instead of milk gives more tang and rise. Savory: drop the sugar to 2 tbsp. Sweeter: up to 2 tbsp honey in the wet mix. Fold in ½ cup shredded cheese or ⅓ cup jalapeños partway through mixing if you want. Dense means overmixed or old baking powder; no crust means the pan wasn't hot.

Recipe by John Kanell (Preppy Kitchen): video · written recipe (the source for every amount). The page says 9" pan but mentions a 10" skillet once; in a 10" or 9×9, check at 15 min.

Classic eggnog

Lance · Wed night → serve Thuno cooking8–10 servingschill overnight
Two things to sort before Wednesday. The liquor comes from a liquor store, not Central Market, and Texas liquor stores are closed on Thanksgiving. The eggs stay raw, so for a crowd use pasteurized-in-shell eggs (Davidson's Safest Choice is the common brand) and skip serving it to anyone pregnant or immunocompromised.
IngredientAmountNote
Large eggs8Separated
Whole milk4 cups~950 mL
Heavy cream2 cups~475 mL
Granulated sugar⅔ cup~133 g
Bourbon4 ozA wheated bourbon (e.g. Maker's Mark) adds sweetness
Dark rum4 ozNot spiced rum
Cognac or apple brandy4 oz
Whole nutmegto grateFor the top of each glass
  1. Separate the eggs. Beat the whites on high (stand or hand mixer) until they look like soft-peak meringue.
  2. In a large bowl, whisk the yolks with the milk, cream, sugar, bourbon, rum and Cognac until smooth.
  3. Gently fold the beaten whites into the yolk mixture until combined.
  4. Pour into a large serving bowl or pitchers, cover, and refrigerate overnight.
  5. To serve, stir it back together, ladle a few ounces into each glass and grate fresh nutmeg on top.
Notes. It keeps in the fridge up to two weeks and gets better with a few days' rest, so making it as early as Monday is fine. Serves 8–10; for 12 with seconds, make it 1.5× (12 eggs, 6 cups milk, 3 cups cream, 1 cup sugar, 6 oz of each spirit). A NYT commenter's smoother version: cook the yolks, milk and sugar in a double boiler until it coats a spoon, cool, then add the whites.

Recipe: Classic Eggnog by Robert Simonson, NYT Cooking, after Aaron Goldfarb.
